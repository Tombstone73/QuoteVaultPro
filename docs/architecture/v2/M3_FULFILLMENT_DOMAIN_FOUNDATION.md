# M3 Fulfillment and Finance — Slice 1: Fulfillment Domain Foundation

Fulfillment is the canonical owner of completed customer handoffs. It stores an immutable `v2_fulfillment_handoffs` fact for every completed pickup or shipment and immutable `v2_fulfillment_handoff_lines` allocations for the exact OrderLines and quantities handed off. A handoff is completed when inserted; there is no draft, reservation, mutable remaining-quantity field, fulfillment status on Sales, or carrier/provider state in this slice.

Availability is a derived projection per OrderLine: `orderedQuantity - (completedPickupQuantity + completedShipmentQuantity)`. The ordered quantity is read from the tenant-scoped Sales order line at transaction time. Production output, Prepress, Routing, Inventory, and Billing are absent from this calculation by design. Consequently static, resale, and pre-produced items can be picked up or shipped without a Production record, and pickup plus shipment can legitimately complete one line.

`recordPickup` and `recordShipment` are separate M0-coordinated operations with a matching business request identity, canonical command fingerprint, durable replay, conflict detection, truthful Principal attribution, and concise shared Audit events. They share one handoff/line model, so their quantities cannot diverge. The terminal result is the seam for a future Billing/reconciliation/outbox consumer; Slice 1 emits no speculative downstream event.

For each completion PostgreSQL locks the open Sales order header and all requested OrderLines in stable line-ID order, calculates completed quantities from immutable allocations, and rejects allocations exceeding the remaining ordered quantity. The order lock is the stable serialization anchor for pickup-vs-pickup and pickup-vs-shipment races, including an otherwise empty allocation history. Tenant-scoped composite foreign keys keep headers and allocations tied to the same Order and line; restrictive delete actions preserve history. Database triggers make completed handoffs and allocations append-only and reject raw inserts on cancelled orders.

Cancellation is currently an Order-level Sales fact. Prior handoffs remain queryable after cancellation, while new completion is rejected. Current V2 Sales has no per-line cancellation or fulfillable/structural-parent contract, so this slice intentionally treats each persisted positive Sales OrderLine as a potentially fulfillable business line and does not invent a parent/child, service/fee, or line-cancellation policy. Those policies require a later explicit Sales read contract. Sales quantity reduction/deletion after a handoff likewise requires a future Sales-side fulfillment-aware edit policy; restrictive allocation FKs block destructive line deletion.

Routing remains the owner of internal shop movement; Fulfillment neither reads nor advances route state. Production owns work/output/satisfaction and cannot cap handoff quantity. Shipping/carrier selection, labels, tracking, webhooks, address validation, and provider recovery are deferred to a future Shipping/Integrations boundary. Billing, payments, refunds, invoice issuance, inventory consumption/reservation, corrections/reversals, and the Fulfillment UI/API integration are deferred.

Validation evidence for this slice is recorded by the focused contracts, migration contract test, guarded PostgreSQL clone rehearsal, migration preflight, V2 type check, import-boundary check, and `git diff --check`. The clone rehearsal seeds only Sales facts—no Production output—and covers partial/repeated/mixed handoffs, independent lines, exact completion, rejection, tenant/authority/idempotency behavior, cancellation history, concurrency, immutable SQL history, attribution/audit, and rollback injection.
